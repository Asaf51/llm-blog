A long-form, illustrated guide

How LLMs Really Work

From floating-point bits and GPU memory to attention, training, reasoning, quantization and serving. One idea at a time, down to the bytes and FLOPs.

What this is

This is a book-length blog that explains large language models completely: what they compute, how they are trained, and exactly what happens on the hardware when you send a prompt. It assumes you know how computers work at a low level (caches, memory hierarchy, virtual memory, SIMD, floating point) but nothing about machine learning.

Every chapter follows the same pattern: the problem first, then the idea in plain words, then the exact mechanics with tensor shapes, then the low-level reality in bytes, FLOPs and bandwidth, with real numbers from real models and GPUs. Diagrams are interactive: step through animations with ← → or the Play button, and turn the knobs in the widgets.

How to read it

Read in order the first time. Each concept is explained in exactly one chapter and linked from everywhere else, so later chapters build on earlier ones without repeating them. If you are in a hurry: Chapters 1, 5, 6, 11 and 12 are the core of "how inference works"; Chapter 16 ties everything together and ends with a cheat sheet, glossary and self-test.
